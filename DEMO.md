# Decoy: the three-minute stage script and runbook

Event: xAI Grokathon, San Francisco, 8 August 2026. Presenter: the host. Product: X Arcade,
first cartridge Decoy. The demo runs in `ARCADE_MODE=demo`, fully offline. Every number
said out loud has a file path in the numbers card at the bottom of this file. Anything
not on that card does not get said.

Timing honesty, stated once here and volunteered on stage before anyone asks: the shell
was built the day before the event, in public commits on github.com/arunshar/x-arcade-grokathon.
What is from the event day is the same-day round refresh (if it ran, see the checkbox in
preflight) and whatever the day queue shipped. Never blur this.

## Preflight, before walking anywhere near the stage

1. Hotspot on. Laptop joined to it. `ipconfig getifaddr en0` for the laptop IP.
2. `./run.sh --host 0.0.0.0` from the repo root. The `--host` flag is what lets a phone
   reach the laptop. Without it the server binds localhost only.
3. `curl http://localhost:8787/health` returns `{"mode": "demo", "rounds_available": 5}`.
   If the count is not 5, stop and find out which round the gates changed their mind on.
4. `python integration_check.py` prints ALL CHECKS PASSED and the zero-network-egress
   line. Run it once at home and once at the venue.
5. Sound check: join a round in one tab, hear the host intro line. Know where the SND
   button is.
6. Volunteer kit ready: a card with the hotspot name, password, and
   `http://<laptop-ip>:8787`, plus your own phone already on the join screen as the
   handoff fallback.
7. Room code chosen and typed into the laptop tab: use `GROK`.
8. Checkbox, fill in on the day: [ ] same-day round refresh ran and passed the gates.
   If checked, the rounds on screen are from this morning's threads. If not, they are
   from 7 August, dated and linked, and you say so.

## The cold open (0:00 to 0:15, spoken)

Screen: the lobby, room code already entered on the laptop.

Say, word for word:

"X suspends around two hundred bots a minute. That count came from X's own head of
product. Every reply on this app is already a guessing game, human or machine, and
everyone plays it badly for free. We turned it into an actual game. This is X Arcade."

Rule for the first sentence: say the figure only if the URL card for it is on your
phone (see claim sources at the bottom). If the card is missing, open with "X is
fighting a bot flood every minute of the day" and keep moving.

## Beat by beat

### 0:15 to 0:40. A volunteer joins on their phone

Do: pick a judge or a front-row volunteer. Hand them the kit card. They join the
hotspot, open the URL, type a name and the room code `GROK`, and hit JOIN ROOM.

Say while they type: "No install, no account. A room code and a browser. The server is
on this laptop and the whole thing runs offline, which matters in about two minutes."

If their phone stalls for more than 20 seconds, hand them your pre-loaded phone instead.
Do not debug on stage.

### 0:40 to 1:15. The round

The round starts the moment the second player joins. The host voice line plays on its
own: "Welcome to the arcade. Tonight, one of the players at this cabinet is not a
player at all." The 30-second clock starts.

On screen: a real post from a real thread with its live reply set. First round served
is the crypto round from the Cointelegraph thread, because the gates removed the ai
round from the queue (that beat comes at 1:50).

Say: "This is a real post, pulled from a real thread, link preserved. Four of these
five replies are real people, read verbatim. One was written by Grok in the register
of the thread. Thirty seconds. Tap the machine."

Both of you guess. Let the clock pressure do the entertaining. If the volunteer
hesitates, needle gently: "The crowd can shout. The crowd is usually wrong."

### 1:15 to 1:40. The reveal

All guesses in, or the clock runs out. The decoy flips red, the real author handles
appear on the human replies, the rationale prints under the decoy, and the winner
banner lands. The reveal voice line plays: "Hands off the buttons. The decoy was..."

Say: "There is the machine, and there is why it read as machine. Real handles restored
on the humans. First correct tap took the round." If the volunteer won, give them the
moment. If the house won, say: "Both wrong. The machine walks free. That is the point
of the game."

### 1:40 to 1:50. The share card

The wanted-poster share card renders on the reveal screen.

Say: "Winner gets a share card, generated by Grok Imagine through the same pipeline,
one tap from being a post. Posting back to X is staged today, not live, and I will say
exactly that every time it comes up."

### 1:50 to 2:00. The safety chip

Point at the round counter or say it cold:

"One of the six rounds we pulled never made it into the game. The gates threw it out,
two failures, G_SOURCE and G_URL. Five deterministic gates run on every round before it
serves, no model in the loop, no override, fail closed. A round that fails is simply
never seen."

If the same-day refresh ran, say "pulled this morning." If not, say "pulled yesterday."
Check the preflight checkbox before you are on stage, not during this sentence.

### 2:00 to 2:15. The Crux tease

Say: "Decoy is cartridge one. Cartridge two, Crux, is sketched in the repo. The point
is the spine, not the game: rounds in, gates in front, two-player rooms, share card
out. The contract file makes a new cartridge roughly a two-hour build, and that estimate
is the honest pitch for what a themed cartridge three looks like by tonight."

Spoken only. There is no Crux tab in the UI, so do not click anything.

### 2:15 to 3:00. The close

Say: "Every big platform grows a games layer eventually. X never has, and the AI-built
game promised for this year has not shipped. X Arcade is that missing layer: games
generated from the live feed, playable in seconds, shareable as posts, brand-safe by
construction because the gates run before anything serves. And Decoy has a second life.
Every guess is a human labeling what machine text reads like. Aggregate that across
millions of rounds and you have the training signal X's bot defense wants. That last
part is a thesis, not a measurement. The game you just played is not a thesis. Thanks."

The platform claims in the close follow the same URL-card rule as the cold open. With
no cards in pocket, cut the first two sentences and open on "X Arcade is a games layer
for X."

## The fallback tree

- Venue wifi dead: nothing changes. Demo mode is offline by design and the integration
  trace proves a full two-player round with zero network egress. The phone join rides
  the hotspot, not venue wifi.
- Browser audio blocked: hit SND OFF and speak the host lines yourself. Texts, exactly
  as the mp3s say them:
  - intro: "Welcome to the arcade. Tonight, one of the players at this cabinet is not
    a player at all."
  - round: "Four humans. One machine. Thirty seconds."
  - reveal: "Hands off the buttons. The decoy was..."
  - win: "Got it! The machine never stood a chance."
  - lose: "Wrong! The machine walks free. House wins."
- Phone will not join: hand over your pre-loaded phone. If that fails too, two tabs on
  the laptop, volunteer drives one of them. The game plays identically.
- Total catastrophe, server will not start: open `web/index.html?mock=1` in any
  browser, straight from disk. Mock mode drives every screen with no server at all,
  and its first round is built on a real post captured by the 7 August probe.

## The five hardest questions

**"Did you build this today?"**
"The shell, no, and I want you to check. I built it yesterday, the commits are public
on github.com/arunshar/x-arcade-grokathon with timestamps. What is from today: the round refresh
from this morning's threads if it ran, the live voice wiring, and whatever else the day
queue shipped, and I will tell you exactly which items those are. The architecture doc
is the real answer to the question: CONTRACT.md defines the round spine, and a new
cartridge lands in about two hours. Twelve hours was never enough to build the shell
and the game and the gates honestly, so I spent yesterday on the shell and today on
what only today's data makes possible." Adjust the middle sentence to what actually
shipped. Never claim an unshipped queue item.

**"This trains people to accuse humans of being bots."**
"Three design choices answer that. Rounds only come from public threads on large
accounts, and real author handles are hidden during guessing, so nobody is ever asked
to judge a named person. The imposter is always disclosed at the reveal, every round
ends with the truth, so the game trains calibration and shows players when they are
wrong, which is the opposite of a drive-by accusation. And the aggregate signal is
about machine text patterns, what generated writing reads like, never a verdict on any
individual account."

**"Why is this a product and not a feature?"**
"A feature is one game. This is a surface. The cartridge contract, the room
infrastructure, the safety gates, the share loop, and the sponsor layer are all
game-agnostic. Decoy is cartridge one, Crux is sketched, and a themed cartridge is
roughly a two-hour build on the same spine. The platform precedent is that a games layer
becomes its own economy, and X has none today." If pressed for the precedent, the
WeChat mini-games claim needs its URL card, same rule as everything else.

**"What does Grok uniquely add?"**
"Four surfaces from one stack, against data only this platform has first-party.
Live search reads the real thread grounded, with post URLs preserved in every round
file. Grok 4.5 writes the imposter in the register of that exact thread, fresh, which
is the whole game. Imagine renders the share card. Grok voice hosts the cabinet. Try
building this on another stack and you have no live X data and no right to it. And the
loop points back at the platform: every guess is a labeled human judgment about what
reads as machine written, which is aggregate signal a bot defense wants. That flywheel
is a thesis, and I label it one every time."

**"How does this make money?"**
"Two native lanes, both projections, no revenue measured. Sponsored arenas: a brand
skins a topic arena, light touch, and the safety gates run before any arena serves, so
a brand never sits next to a round that failed screening. Every sponsor name in the
demo config is fictional and the module says so. Second lane, the share card is the ad
unit: a winner posts the card carrying the arena mark, and distribution rides the
player's own follower graph. Any figure I attach to either lane today is illustrative
and I will call it illustrative in the same breath."

## The QR open (updated 7 Aug, supersedes the cold open above)

Slide 4 stays on screen while people settle. It shows the QR only.

Spoken: "Before I explain anything, take out your phone and scan this. Pick a name. You are now in
the game." Wait ten seconds, watch the player list grow on the projector, then start the round from
the host device. The crowd plays the same round you narrate. The reveal leaderboard on the big
screen is the applause line.

Mechanics: the hosted app is https://YOUR-SPACE.hf.space and the QR resolves to
`/?room=GROK`, which prefills the room so a phone joins in one tap. Room GROK is an arena: any
number of players, only the first joiner (you, joined as HOST before the talk) can start rounds, and
tapping NEXT on a phone does nothing. Open the Space ten minutes before the slot so the container is
warm. If venue internet dies entirely, fall back to the laptop server plus hotspot with room codes,
and the offline demo mode carries the content either way.
